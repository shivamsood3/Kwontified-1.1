Holiday 2026: the season now starts in October

By Elaine Kwon, Co-Founder and Managing Partner. Published Nov 5, 2026.

Adobe expects US shoppers to spend $95.8 billion online in October, up 8%, in a month that sits outside its holiday window and still equals about 35% of the season it forecasts, PPC Land reported. Retailers moved major events into October and early November, and shoppers spend heavily before the traditional season begins.

So the 2026 holiday season now starts in October. Brands should plan a ten-week season with a different job for each phase, rather than concentrating everything on Black Friday and Cyber Monday.

Where the holiday 2026 season stands

Adobe’s headline forecast is a record. US online spending is expected to reach $275.1 billion between November 1 and December 31, up 6.7%, Adobe said. Across October to December, its forecast report puts online spending at $370.9 billion, up 7%, and describes October as a step-change in spending.

Cyber Week is still the peak. The five days from Thanksgiving to Cyber Monday are forecast to drive $47.5 billion, up 7.4%, or 17.3% of the season, and in 2026 the whole stretch falls inside November, with Cyber Monday on November 30, PPC Land reported. The two biggest days of that week are converging. Cyber Monday is forecast at $15.1 billion, up 6.2%, PPC Land reported, while Black Friday is forecast at $12.9 billion, up 9.2%, according to Relevant Audience. Black Friday is growing faster than Cyber Monday, though Cyber Monday remains the largest day.

The holiday basket mixes gifts and essentials. Adobe expects electronics, apparel, furniture, grocery, toys and cosmetics to lead online holiday spending, with grocery forecast to grow 10.3%, Digital Commerce 360 reported. Buy now, pay later services are expected to drive $21.3 billion in spending across November and December, up 6.6%, according to the report.

The October event has already delivered. Prime Big Deal Days brought $9.86 billion in US online spending across retailers over October 6 and 7, up 8.5%, in Adobe figures carried by Digital Commerce 360. Rivals also set their dates early. Walmart announced early Black Friday deals from November 12 to 15, Black Friday deals from November 23 to 29 and Cyber Monday deals online through December 2, RTTNews reported.

How the season moved into October

Retailers added events to the calendar. Amazon’s October sale, Walmart’s and Target’s competing events in the same week, and Walmart’s early Black Friday window in mid-November turned a single weekend into a run of events. Shoppers followed the deals: with discounts available from early October, many of them began buying earlier, especially for everyday items and early gifts.

Gift decisions still come later. In Amazon’s October event, only 20% of shoppers said they bought holiday gifts, and 56% were not yet sure what they wanted for the holidays, Numerator found in its tracker. The season starts earlier for spending than for gift decisions.

Inside the peak, the weekend matters again. With Black Friday growing faster than Cyber Monday, brands that hold most of their budget for the Monday may run short when demand arrives earlier in the weekend. Payment options stretch spending too, since buy now, pay later lets shoppers spread larger purchases across weeks, which supports bigger baskets during the peak. And the calendar compresses the peak: with Cyber Monday on November 30, all of Cyber Week falls in November, which makes November an even bigger month for many brands.

Put together, a season that runs from early October to late December has phases with different jobs. Treating it as one push around Black Friday wastes money early and misses demand late. Not every category follows this pattern, though. Some products sell mostly in December, and some brands see little October demand, so the phases should be set from each brand’s own sales history.

Four phases, each with its own job

Take a toy brand that, in past years, held most of its budget for Black Friday week. In 2026, it used October to test prices on its best sellers, started gift advertising when Walmart’s early deals opened in mid-November, concentrated spend in Cyber Week and kept a smaller budget for December gift buyers. Its stock plan followed the same phases: an October test, an early November build, a Cyber Week peak and a December finish.

Brands should plan October, early November, Cyber Week and December as separate phases, each with its own goal. Price, stock and ad results from October show which products can carry the season, and they should decide which products lead Cyber Week. Gifting starts before Thanksgiving: with Walmart opening early deals on November 12, gift advertising should start well before Black Friday.

The five days from Thanksgiving to Cyber Monday remain the largest share of the season and deserve the largest daily budgets. December still needs a reserve. Late gift buyers shop in December, especially for fast delivery, and a small budget keeps best sellers visible through the final weeks. Inventory should be placed for each phase, not only for the Cyber Week peak, so best sellers do not run out before December.

Questions for the holiday plan

  • Does each phase, from October to December, have its own budget and goal?
  • Which products did October’s price, stock and ad results pick to lead Cyber Week?
  • Will gift advertising start before Walmart’s early deals open on November 12?
  • Is there a December reserve, and is stock placed so best sellers last until then?

For the planning meeting, the first question is whether the holiday plan still centers on one weekend. If it does, splitting it into four phases with separate budgets is the place to start, and planning those phases across Amazon, Walmart and Target is what we help brands do for the full holiday season.
